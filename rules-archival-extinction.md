A tree leaf may be marked `Archival Extinction` only with hard, verified confirmation that earlier records for the line no longer survive anywhere. That requires exhaustive research into every avenue, however obscure: paid repositories, specialized archives, church-book inventories, parish card indexes, notarial registers, tax, manorial and court records, and documented regional losses (the Thirty Years' War, the 1922 Irish Public Record Office fire, lost parish registers). Never mark a line extinct because of an unresearched gap, a lack of indexes, or an ordinary brick wall. The start date of a register shows where one series begins; it does not prove that no other evidence survives.

A mark needs the owner's approval and the reasons documented, with citations to the supporting sources (archive inventories, register catalogues, diocesan guides, negative-search reports, records of the loss), in three places: `tree.ged` (`1 EVEN` / `2 TYPE Archival Extinction` / `2 NOTE` reason / `2 SOUR` sources), a dossier in `sources/`, and the Archival Limits Register in `open-questions.md`. The viewer shows marked people with a red LIMIT badge.
